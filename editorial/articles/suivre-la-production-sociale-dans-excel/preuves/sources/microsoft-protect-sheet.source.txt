<div class="learnArticleContent">
	<p><a id="__goback"></a>
To prevent other users from accidentally or deliberately changing, moving, or deleting data in a worksheet, you can lock the cells on your Excel worksheet and then protect the sheet with a password. Say you own the team status report worksheet, where you want team members to add data in specific cells only and not be able to modify anything else. With worksheet protection, you can make only certain parts of the sheet editable and users will not be able to modify data in any other region in the sheet. </p>
<div class="IMPORTANT">
<p>Important</p>
<p>Worksheet level protection isn't intended as a security feature. It simply prevents users from modifying locked cells within the worksheet. Protecting a worksheet is not the same as protecting an Excel file or a workbook with a password. See below for more information:</p>
<ul>
<li>To lock your file so that other users can't open it, see <a href="get-started/protect-an-excel-file" data-linktype="relative-path">Protect an Excel file</a>.</li>
<li>To prevent users from adding, modifying, moving, copying, or hiding/unhiding sheets within a workbook, see <a href="protect-a-workbook" data-linktype="relative-path">Protect a workbook</a>.</li>
<li>To know the difference between protecting your Excel file, workbook, or a worksheet see <a href="protection-and-security-in-excel" data-linktype="relative-path">Protection and security in Excel</a>.</li>
<li>Worksheet protection is not available for U.S. Government Community Cloud High (GCCH) or Department of Defense (DOD) environments.</li>
</ul>
</div>

<p>The following sections describe how to protect and unprotect a worksheet in Excel for Windows.</p>
<h2 id="choose-what-cell-elements-to-lock">Choose what cell elements to lock</h2>
<p>Here's what you can lock in an unprotected sheet:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Formulas</strong>: If you don't want other users to see your formulas, you can hide them from being seen in cells or the Formula bar. For more information, see <a href="display-or-hide-formulas" data-linktype="relative-path">Display or hide formulas</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Ranges</strong>: You can enable users to work in specific ranges within a protected sheet. For more information, see <a href="get-started/lock-or-unlock-specific-areas-of-a-protected-worksheet" data-linktype="relative-path">Lock or unlock specific areas of a protected worksheet</a>.</li>
</ul>
<div class="NOTE">
<p>Note</p>
<p>ActiveX controls, form controls, shapes, charts, SmartArt, Sparklines, Slicers, Timelines, to name a few, are already locked when you add them to a spreadsheet. But the lock will work only when you enable sheet protection. See the subsequent section for more information on how to enable sheet protection.</p>
</div>
<h2 id="enable-worksheet-protection">Enable worksheet protection</h2>
<p>Worksheet protection is a two-step process: the first step is to unlock cells that others can edit, and then you can protect the worksheet with or without a password.</p>
<p><strong>Step 1: Unlock any cells that needs to be editable</strong></p>
<ol>
<li><p>In your Excel file, select the worksheet tab that you want to protect.</p>
</li>
<li><p>Select the cells that others can edit.</p>
<div class="TIP">
<p>Tip</p>
<p>You can select multiple, non-contiguous cells by pressing Ctrl+Left-Click.</p>
</div>
</li>
<li><p>Right-click anywhere in the sheet and select <strong>Format Cells</strong> (or use <strong>Ctrl+1</strong>, or <strong>Command+1</strong> on the Mac), and then go to the <strong>Protection</strong> tab and clear <strong>Locked</strong>.</p>
<p><span class="mx-imgBorder">
<img src="media/protection-tab-in-format-cells.png" alt="Protection Tab under the Format Cells dialog box" data-linktype="relative-path">
</span>
</p>
</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Step 2: Protect the worksheet</strong></p>
<p>Next, select the actions that users should be allowed to take on the sheet, such as insert or delete columns or rows, edit objects, sort, or use AutoFilter, to name a few. Additionally, you can also specify a password to lock your worksheet. A password prevents other people from removing the worksheet protection—it needs to be entered to unprotect the sheet.</p>
<p>Given below are the steps to protect your sheet.</p>
<ol>
<li><p>On the <strong>Review</strong> tab, select <strong>Protect Sheet</strong>.</p>

</span>
</p>

<h2 id="unprotect-an-excel-worksheet">Unprotect an Excel worksheet</h2>
<p>To unprotect a sheet, follow these steps:</p>
<ol>
<li>Go to the worksheet you want to unprotect.</li>
<li>Go to <strong>File</strong> &gt; <strong>Info</strong> &gt; <strong>Protect</strong> &gt; <strong>Unprotect Sheet</strong>, or from the <strong>Review</strong> tab &gt; <strong>Changes</strong> &gt; <strong>Unprotect Sheet</strong>.</li>
<li>If the sheet is protected with a password, then enter the password in the <strong>Unprotect Sheet</strong> dialog box and select <strong>OK</strong>.</li>
</ol>

<td>Delete rows.<br/><strong>Note:</strong> If <strong>Delete rows</strong> is protected and <strong>Insert rows</strong> is not protected, a user can insert rows but cannot delete them.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Sort</strong></td>
<td>Use any commands to sort data (<strong>Data</strong> tab, <strong>Sort &amp; Filter</strong> group).<br/><strong>Note:</strong> Users can't sort ranges that contain locked cells on a protected worksheet, regardless of this setting.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Use AutoFilter</strong></td>
<td>Use the drop-down arrows to change the filter on ranges when AutoFilters are applied.<br/><strong>Note:</strong> Users cannot apply or remove AutoFilter on a protected worksheet, regardless of this setting.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Use PivotTable reports</strong></td>
<td>Format, change the layout, refresh, or otherwise modify PivotTable reports, or create new reports.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Edit objects</strong></td>
<td>Doing any of the following:<br/><ul><li>Make changes to graphic objects including maps, embedded charts, shapes, text boxes, and controls that you did not unlock before you protected the worksheet. For example, if a worksheet has a button that runs a macro, you can select the button to run the macro, but you cannot delete the button.</li><li>Make any changes, such as formatting, to an embedded chart. The chart continues to be updated when you change its source data.</li><li>Add or edit notes.</li></ul></td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Edit scenarios</strong></td>
<td>View scenarios that you have hidden, making changes to scenarios that you have prevented changes to, and deleting these scenarios. Users can change the values in the changing cells, if the cells are not protected, and add new scenarios.</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p><strong>Notes</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>If you don't want other users to see your formulas, you can hide them from being seen in cells or the Formula bar. For more information, see <a href="display-or-hide-formulas" data-linktype="relative-path">Display or hide formulas.</a></li>
<li>ActiveX controls, form controls, shapes, charts, SmartArt, Sparklines, Slicers, Timelines, and so on, are already locked when you add them to a spreadsheet. But the lock works only when you enable sheet protection. For more information, see <a href="protect-controls-and-linked-cells-on-a-worksheet" data-linktype="relative-path">Protect controls and linked cells on a worksheet</a>.</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="unprotect-a-sheet">Unprotect a sheet</h2>
<p>There are two ways to unprotect a sheet, disable it or pause it.</p>
<p><strong>Disable protection</strong></p>
<ol>
<li><p>Select <strong>Review</strong> &gt; <strong>Manage Protection</strong>.</p>
</li>
<li><p>To turn off protection, In the <strong>Manage Protection</strong> task pane, turn off <strong>Protect sheet</strong>.</p>
<p><span class="mx-imgBorder">
<img src="media/excelprotectsheetoff.png" alt="Turning sheet protection off" data-linktype="relative-path">
</span>
</p>
</li>
</ol>
<p><strong>Pause protection</strong></p>
<p>Pausing protection turns off protection for the current editing session while maintaining the protection for other users in the workbook. For example, you can pause protection to edit a locked range but maintain protection for other users.</p>
<ol>
<li><p>To pause sheet protection, select <strong>Review</strong> &gt; <strong>Pause Protection</strong>.</p>
<p><span class="mx-imgBorder">
<img src="media/excelpauseprotection.png" alt="Pausing sheet protection" data-linktype="relative-path">
</span>
</p>
<p><strong>Note</strong> If the sheet has a protection password, you must enter that password to pause protection.</p>
</li>
<li><p>To resume sheet protection, select <strong>Review</strong> &gt; <strong>Resume Protection</strong>.</p>
<p><span class="mx-imgBorder">
<img src="media/excelresumeprotection.png" alt="Resuming sheet protection" data-linktype="relative-path">
</span>
</p>
</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="how-can-i-tell-if-a-sheet-is-protected-1">How can I tell if a sheet is protected?</h2>
<p>At the bottom of the sheet, the sheet tab displays a locked icon if the sheet is protected (<strong>Sheet1</strong>), and an unlocked icon if it is paused (<strong>Sheet2</strong>).</p>
<p><span class="mx-imgBorder">
<img src="media/excelprotectionsheetonetwo.png" alt="Revealing sheet protection" data-linktype="relative-path">
